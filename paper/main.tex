\begin{abstract}
Automated plagiarism detection demands robust semantic evaluation beyond surface-level lexical matching. Large language models (LLMs) synthesize evasive paraphrases capable of bypassing conventional continuous similarity filters. This manuscript investigates the structural resilience of modern retrieval and embedding-based plagiarism defenses against adaptive, detector-aware adversarial paraphrase attacks. We formulate a Leave-One-Out (LOO) saliency-guided paraphrase algorithm that iteratively optimizes text perturbations directly against the continuous scalar similarity feedback of target defense systems under a strict black-box query budget. We evaluate this mechanism against a five-architecture defense matrix comprising Sentence-BERT, SimCSE, BMX Hybrid, ColBERT multi-vector late-interaction, and Longformer. To prevent semantic collapse, our pipeline enforces an architecturally independent Natural Language Inference (NLI) fidelity gate. Empirical results across academic (PlagBench), obfuscated (PADBen), legal, scientific (SciDocs), and journalistic (SemEval-2022) corpora demonstrate that pooled-vector encoders suffer high evasion rates (FPER up to 88.3\%), whereas lexical-semantic hybrid and multi-vector architectures exhibit superior adversarial resistance. We present the unified $5 \times 5$ cross-architecture transferability matrix and provide quantitative comparisons against prior baseline detectors.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Plagiarism detection has evolved from verbatim string comparison toward deep contextual and semantic evaluation. Modern content deduplication, academic integrity networks, and intellectual property repositories depend fundamentally on continuous retrieval and similarity-based architectures \cite{devlin2019bert, reimers2019sbert, gao2021simcse}. These systems leverage dense contextual embeddings generated by transformer neural encoders to project source documents $\mathbf{x}$ and suspect candidate texts $\mathbf{\tilde{x}}$ into high-dimensional latent metric spaces, classifying plagiarism when continuous similarity scores exceed calibrated decision thresholds $\tau$.

However, the rapid advancement of generative Large Language Models (LLMs) exposes severe vulnerabilities within continuous similarity defenses. Unlike static automated synonym replacers, an adaptive adversary can query a black-box similarity oracle iteratively, leveraging continuous score feedback to guide generative rewriting toward minimum detection regions \cite{waghela2024sassp, zha2025padben}. This creates an optimization loop where syntactic structures, phrasing, and lexical selections are systematically altered while preserving underlying factual meaning. While zero-shot discrete classifier detectors (e.g., DetectGPT \cite{mitchell2023detectgpt}, watermarking schemes \cite{kirchenbauer2023watermark}) have been extensively stress-tested, the structural stability of continuous retrieval-based plagiarism defenses against iterative, detector-aware adversarial attacks remains largely unquantified in the literature.

Deployed defense architectures exhibit substantial structural diversity. Sentence-BERT ($\mathcal{D}_1$) compresses sequences via siamese mean token pooling \cite{reimers2019sbert}. SimCSE ($\mathcal{D}_2$) constructs isotropic representations via contrastive InfoNCE learning over dropout masks \cite{gao2021simcse}. BMX ($\mathcal{D}_3$) combines dense semantic similarity with entropy-weighted lexical overlap to prevent pure embedding manipulation \cite{li2024bmx, robertson2009bm25}. ColBERT ($\mathcal{D}_4$) employs token-level multi-vector late-interaction matching via a MaxSim operator \cite{khattab2020colbert, mysore2022scientific}. Longformer ($\mathcal{D}_5$) utilizes localized sliding-window and sparse global attention to eliminate sequence truncation across long-form documents \cite{beltagy2020longformer, jha2023longform}.

In this paper, we develop an adaptive, detector-aware paraphrase attack framework operating under a strict black-box query budget ($\mathcal{B} = 50$). Our algorithm combines Leave-One-Out (LOO) syntactic span ablation with feedback-driven rejection sampling and an architecturally independent Natural Language Inference (NLI) cross-encoder fidelity verification gate \cite{he2021deberta, zhang2019bertscore}. 

The primary contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Adaptive Saliency-Guided Attack Algorithm}: We formulate a detector-aware paraphrase attack that identifies high-attribution syntactic spans via Leave-One-Out ablation and applies targeted LLM perturbation with rejection sampling.
    \item \textbf{Five-Architecture Defense Benchmark Matrix}: We systematically evaluate five representative defense paradigms ($\mathcal{D}_1$: SBERT, $\mathcal{D}_2$: SimCSE, $\mathcal{D}_3$: BMX Hybrid, $\mathcal{D}_4$: ColBERT Multi-Vector, $\mathcal{D}_5$: Longformer) in their pre-trained configurations.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Architecture Transferability Matrix}: We compute the empirical $5 \times 5$ transferability matrix $\mathbf{T} \in \mathbb{R}^{5 \times 5}$, quantifying how adversarial rewrites optimized against source defense $\mathcal{D}_i$ transfer zero-shot to target defense $\mathcal{D}_j$.
    \item \textbf{Independent Semantic Fidelity Gating}: We decouple mathematical evasion success from semantic degradation by enforcing an architecturally independent non-BERT NLI cross-encoder verification constraint ($\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{\hat{x}}) \ge \theta_{\text{fid}}$).
    \item \textbf{Cross-Domain Generalization}: We perform comprehensive empirical evaluations across five distinct text registers comprising 120 standardized benchmark pairs spanning academic, obfuscated, legal, scientific, and journalistic domains.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

We contextualize our methodology across three core literature domains: transformer-based semantic representations, architectural innovations for retrieval indexing, and adversarial attacks on natural language processing systems.

\subsection{Transformer-Based Semantic Representations}
Contextual representation learning commenced with masked language modeling in BERT \cite{devlin2019bert} and RoBERTa \cite{liu2019roberta}. Sentence-BERT adapted these backbones using Siamese and triplet network configurations to generate semantically meaningful sentence embeddings compared via cosine distance \cite{reimers2019sbert}. SimCSE advanced representation isotropy by demonstrating that contrastive learning over identical sentences with independent dropout masks yields competitive sentence representations without labeled NLI supervision \cite{gao2021simcse}. Subsequent models expanded multi-task contrastive fine-tuning across massive sentence pairs \cite{ni2022sentence}.

\subsection{Architectural Developments for Retrieval \& Matching}
Dense embedding models frequently suffer from embedding anisotropy and loss of fine-grained lexical keywords \cite{li2024bmx, robertson2009bm25}. Hybrid retrieval architectures like BMX fuse dense semantic representations with inverse document frequency (IDF) and entropy-weighted lexical overlap, resolving keyword mismatch failures \cite{li2024bmx, yoo2023patent}. For domain-specific document matching, multi-vector models such as ColBERT retain all token embeddings and execute token-to-token late-interaction MaxSim alignments, preserving granular context in complex scientific and technical texts \cite{khattab2020colbert, mysore2022scientific, santhanam2022colbertv2}. For long-form texts such as court jurisprudence and commercial contracts, Longformer replaces standard $O(N^2)$ quadratic self-attention with sparse sliding-window attention, modeling sequences up to 4,096 tokens without truncation artifacts \cite{beltagy2020longformer, oliveira2022legal, jha2023longform}. Cross-lingual news similarity pipelines further incorporate named-entity extraction to align journalistic discourse \cite{goel2022wolfies}.

\subsection{Adversarial Paraphrasing and AI-Text Detectors}
Adversarial NLP evaluates the robustness of classifiers under targeted input perturbations \cite{morris2020textattack, waghela2024sassp}. Saliency guidance algorithms compute input token attribution scores to perturb solely the highest-influence tokens, achieving superior query efficiency compared to random sampling \cite{waghela2024sassp}. Standardized benchmarks have emerged to evaluate detection filters: PADBen establishes a five-tier obfuscation taxonomy ranging from lexical substitution to multi-hop structural transformations \cite{zha2025padben}; PlagBench supplies large-scale corpora of LLM-generated verbatim, paraphrased, and summarized plagiarism \cite{lee2025plagbench}. DetectGPT detects machine-generated passages in a zero-shot manner by measuring negative curvature in LLM log-probability spaces \cite{mitchell2023detectgpt, hans2024spotting}. Semantic preservation metrics, such as CPES \cite{alsulami2026cpes} and BERTScore \cite{zhang2019bertscore}, evaluate whether adversarial paraphrases retain semantic equivalence without penalizing valid syntactic variations.

Existing literature predominantly evaluates adversarial evasion against binary AI-text classifier detectors. Continuous retrieval and similarity scoring defenses used in production deduplication remain unbenchmarked against iterative, detector-aware optimization loops.

\subsection{Problem Formulation and Mathematical Notation}
Let $\mathcal{X}$ denote the space of natural language sequences. A reference source document is $\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{X}$ and a suspect document is $\mathbf{\tilde{x}} \in \mathcal{X}$. A continuous defense system is defined as $\mathcal{D} = \{f_\theta, \mathcal{S}, \tau\}$, where $f_\theta : \mathcal{X} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^d$ maps text into embedding space, $\mathcal{S}$ computes similarity, and $\tau$ is the calibrated detection threshold.

The binary classification decision $\hat{y} \in \{0, 1\}$ evaluates as:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{y}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{\tilde{x}}) = \mathbb{1}\big[\, \mathcal{S}\big(f_\theta(\mathbf{x}), f_\theta(\mathbf{\tilde{x}})\big) \ge \tau \,\big]
    \label{eq:classification}
\end{equation}
\notationblock{\textbf{Eq. \eqref{eq:classification} Notations:} $\hat{y}$: Binary plagiarism decision. $\mathbb{1}[\cdot]$: Indicator function. $\mathcal{S}(\cdot, \cdot)$: Continuous similarity function. $\tau$: Plagiarism threshold. $f_\theta$: Neural contextual encoder.}

For flagged pairs ($\hat{y} = 1$), the adversary generates an evasive candidate $\mathbf{x}'$ from candidate distribution $\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{\tilde{x}})$ under optimization constraints:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{x}' = \arg\min_{\mathbf{\hat{x}} \in \mathcal{P}(\mathbf{\tilde{x}})} \mathcal{S}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{\hat{x}}) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{\hat{x}}) \ge \theta_{\text{fid}}, \quad \mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{x}') \le \mathcal{B}
    \label{eq:optimization}
\end{equation}
\notationblock{\textbf{Eq. \eqref{eq:optimization} Notations:} $\mathbf{x}'$: Optimized evasive text. $\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{\tilde{x}})$: Candidate rewrite pool. $\mathcal{F}$: Independent semantic fidelity function. $\theta_{\text{fid}}$: Fidelity threshold limit ($0.75$). $\mathcal{Q}(\mathbf{x}')$: Queries consumed. $\mathcal{B}$: Maximum query budget ($50$).}

\subsection{Defense Architecture Matrix ($\mathcal{D}_1$ to $\mathcal{D}_5$)}
We benchmark five distinct defense oracles in their default pre-trained states:

\textbf{1. Sentence-BERT Bi-Encoder ($\mathcal{D}_1$)}: Projects documents to mean-pooled vectors $\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ \cite{reimers2019sbert}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{u} = \frac{1}{|\mathbf{x}|} \sum_{k=1}^{|\mathbf{x}|} \text{BERT}(\mathbf{x})_k, \quad \mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{D}_1}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{\tilde{x}}) = \frac{\mathbf{u}^\top \mathbf{v}}{\|\mathbf{u}\|_2 \, \|\mathbf{v}\|_2}
    \label{eq:sbert}
\end{equation}

\textbf{2. SimCSE Bi-Encoder ($\mathcal{D}_2$)}: Generates isotropic embeddings via contrastive InfoNCE training over dropout masks $\mathbf{z}, \mathbf{z}'$ \cite{gao2021simcse}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_i = -\log \frac{\exp\big(\mathcal{S}_{\text{cos}}(\mathbf{h}_i^\mathbf{z}, \mathbf{h}_i^{\mathbf{z}'}) / \mathcal{T}\big)}{\sum_{j=1}^N \exp\big(\mathcal{S}_{\text{cos}}(\mathbf{h}_i^\mathbf{z}, \mathbf{h}_j^{\mathbf{z}'}) / \mathcal{T}\big)}, \quad \mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{D}_2}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{\tilde{x}}) = \frac{\mathbf{h}_{\mathbf{x}}^\top \mathbf{h}_{\mathbf{\tilde{x}}}}{\|\mathbf{h}_{\mathbf{x}}\|_2 \|\mathbf{h}_{\mathbf{\tilde{x}}}\|_2}
    \label{eq:simcse}
\end{equation}

\textbf{3. BMX Hybrid Scorer ($\mathcal{D}_3$)}: Combines dense cosine similarity with entropy-weighted lexical overlap \cite{li2024bmx, robertson2009bm25}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{D}_3}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{\tilde{x}}) = \alpha \mathcal{S}_{\text{dense}}(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}) + (1 - \alpha) \mathcal{S}_{\text{lex}}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{\tilde{x}})
    \label{eq:bmx}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{S}_{\text{lex}}$ weights term co-occurrences by their Shannon information entropy $-\log_2 p(w)$, and $\alpha = 0.50$.

\textbf{4. ColBERT Multi-Vector Late Interaction ($\mathcal{D}_4$)}: Retains contextual token embedding matrices $\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{x}} \in \mathbb{R}^{|\mathbf{x}| \times d}$ and calculates similarity using the MaxSim operator \cite{khattab2020colbert, mysore2022scientific}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{D}_4}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{\tilde{x}}) = \frac{1}{|\mathbf{x}|} \sum_{i=1}^{|\mathbf{x}|} \max_{j=1..|\mathbf{\tilde{x}}|} \left( \frac{\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{x}, i}^\top \mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{\tilde{x}}, j}}{\|\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{x}, i}\|_2 \, \|\mathbf{E}_{\mathbf{\tilde{x}}, j}\|_2} \right)
    \label{eq:colbert}
\end{equation}

\textbf{5. Longformer Document Scorer ($\mathcal{D}_5$)}: Employs sliding-window chunked multi-head attention to handle sequences up to 4,096 tokens without sequence truncation \cite{beltagy2020longformer, jha2023longform}.

\subsection{Adaptive Saliency-Guided Attack Algorithm}

The attack framework operates in three tiers: Tier 1 (Static Paraphrase baseline), Tier 2 (Saliency-Guided Leave-One-Out Rejection Sampling), and Tier 3 (Closed-Loop RL Reward Policy).

In Tier 2, suspect candidate $\mathbf{\tilde{x}}$ is segmented into $M$ non-overlapping syntactic spans $\mathcal{W} = \{w_1, w_2, \dots, w_M\}$. The attribution score $\mathcal{I}(w_i)$ measures the drop in similarity upon omitting span $w_i$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{I}(w_i) = \big|\, \mathcal{S}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{\tilde{x}}) - \mathcal{S}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{\tilde{x}} \setminus w_i) \,\big|
    \label{eq:saliency}
\end{equation}

The complete procedural execution of Tier 2 is formalized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:saliency_attack}.

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
\caption{Tier 2 Saliency-Guided Adaptive Paraphrase Attack with Dual-Judge Fidelity Gating}
\label{alg:saliency_attack}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Reference text $\mathbf{x}$, Suspect candidate text $\mathbf{\tilde{x}}$, Defense Oracle $\mathcal{D}$, Fidelity Judge $\mathcal{F}$, Query Budget $\mathcal{B}$, Detection Threshold $\tau$, Fidelity Threshold $\theta_{\text{fid}}$, Candidate Pool Size $K$, Top Spans $k$.
\ENSURE Evasive Paraphrase $\mathbf{x}'$, Final Score $s'$, Fidelity Score $f'$, Evasion Status $\text{is\_fper}$.
\STATE Initialize query counter $q \leftarrow 0$, Best candidate $\mathbf{x}' \leftarrow \mathbf{\tilde{x}}$
\STATE Compute initial baseline score: $s_0 \leftarrow \mathcal{D}.\text{score}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{\tilde{x}})$, $q \leftarrow q + 1$
\STATE Evaluate baseline fidelity: $f_0 \leftarrow \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{\tilde{x}})$
\IF{$s_0 < \tau$ \AND $f_0 \ge \theta_{\text{fid}}$}
    \RETURN $\mathbf{\tilde{x}}, s_0, f_0, \text{TRUE}$
\ENDIF
\STATE $s_{\text{best}} \leftarrow s_0, f_{\text{best}} \leftarrow f_0$
\WHILE{$q < \mathcal{B}$ \AND $s_{\text{best}} \ge \tau$}
    \STATE $\mathcal{W} \leftarrow \text{SyntacticSegment}(\mathbf{x}')$ \COMMENT{Decompose into $M$ spans $\{w_1, \dots, w_M\}$}
    \STATE $\mathcal{A} \leftarrow \emptyset$ \COMMENT{Ablation attribution list}
    \FORALL{$w_i \in \mathcal{W}$}
        \IF{$q \ge \mathcal{B}$} \BREAK \ENDIF
        \STATE $\mathbf{x}_{\setminus w_i} \leftarrow \text{AblateSpan}(\mathcal{W}, i)$
        \STATE $s_{\text{abl}} \leftarrow \mathcal{D}.\text{score}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}_{\setminus w_i})$, $q \leftarrow q + 1$
        \STATE $\mathcal{I}(w_i) \leftarrow |s_{\text{best}} - s_{\text{abl}}|$
        \STATE $\mathcal{A} \leftarrow \mathcal{A} \cup \{(i, \mathcal{I}(w_i))\}$
    \ENDFOR
    \STATE Sort $\mathcal{A}$ in descending order of attribution score $\mathcal{I}(w_i)$
    \STATE $\text{TopIndices} \leftarrow \{i \mid (i, \cdot) \in \text{Top-}k(\mathcal{A})\}$
    \STATE $\mathcal{C}_{\text{raw}} \leftarrow \text{SampleCandidates}(\mathbf{x}', \mathcal{W}, \text{TopIndices}, K)$
    \STATE $\mathcal{C}_{\text{valid}} \leftarrow \emptyset$
    \FORALL{$c_j \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{raw}}$}
        \STATE $f_j \leftarrow \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}, c_j)$
        \IF{$f_j \ge \theta_{\text{fid}}$}
            \STATE $\mathcal{C}_{\text{valid}} \leftarrow \mathcal{C}_{\text{valid}} \cup \{(c_j, f_j)\}$
        \ENDIF
    \ENDFOR
    \IF{$\mathcal{C}_{\text{valid}} = \emptyset$}
        \STATE $\mathcal{C}_{\text{valid}} \leftarrow \{(c_1, \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}, c_1))\}$ \COMMENT{Fallback candidate}
    \ENDIF
    \STATE $\mathcal{C}_{\text{scored}} \leftarrow \emptyset$
    \FORALL{$(c_j, f_j) \in \mathcal{C}_{\text{valid}}$}
        \IF{$q \ge \mathcal{B}$} \BREAK \ENDIF
        \STATE $s_j \leftarrow \mathcal{D}.\text{score}(\mathbf{x}, c_j)$, $q \leftarrow q + 1$
        \STATE $\mathcal{C}_{\text{scored}} \leftarrow \mathcal{C}_{\text{scored}} \cup \{(c_j, s_j, f_j)\}$
    \ENDFOR
    \STATE Select minimum score candidate: $(c^*, s^*, f^*) \leftarrow \arg\min_{(c_j, s_j, f_j)} s_j$
    \IF{$s^* < s_{\text{best}}$}
        \STATE $\mathbf{x}' \leftarrow c^*, s_{\text{best}} \leftarrow s^*, f_{\text{best}} \leftarrow f^*$
    \ENDIF
    \IF{$s_{\text{best}} < \tau$ \AND $f_{\text{best}} \ge \theta_{\text{fid}}$}
        \BREAK \COMMENT{Evasion successful within fidelity boundary}
    \ENDIF
\ENDWHILE
\STATE $\text{is\_fper} \leftarrow (s_{\text{best}} < \tau \land f_{\text{best}} \ge \theta_{\text{fid}})$
\RETURN $\mathbf{x}', s_{\text{best}}, f_{\text{best}}, \text{is\_fper}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Architecturally Independent Semantic Fidelity Gate}
To prevent evaluation circularity caused by shared BERT geometries \cite{zhang2019bertscore}, we employ a dual-judge verification system using an independent non-BERT cross-encoder model (\texttt{cross-encoder/nli-deberta-v3-xsmall}) \cite{he2021deberta}. The fidelity function $\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{\hat{x}})$ evaluates bidirectional Natural Language Inference entailment:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{\hat{x}}) = \frac{1}{2}\Big( P_{\text{NLI}}(\mathbf{x} \implies \mathbf{\hat{x}}) + P_{\text{NLI}}(\mathbf{\hat{x}} \implies \mathbf{x}) \Big)
    \label{eq:fidelity}
\end{equation}
A candidate is rejected if $\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{\hat{x}}) < \theta_{\text{fid}} = 0.75$.

\section{Experimental Results \& Discussion}

\subsection{Experimental Setup and Benchmark Corpus}
Evaluations were executed across our standardized 120-document pair benchmark corpus spanning five distinct stylistic registers ($24$ pairs per domain):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Academic (PlagBench)}: Machine learning, NLP, diffusion models, transformers, and representation theory.
    \item \textbf{Obfuscated (PADBen)}: Multi-level laundering spanning active-passive swaps, nominalization, and clause repackaging.
    \item \textbf{Legal Court Corpus}: Long labor tribunal cases (Article 157), NDAs, commercial contracts, and CERCLA liability.
    \item \textbf{Scientific (SciDocs / CSFCUBE)}: CRISPR gene editing, topological quantum gates, spintronics, and smFRET kinetics.
    \item \textbf{Journalistic News (SemEval-2022 Task 8)}: Macroeconomic policy, satellite launches, semiconductor subsidies, and antitrust litigation.
\end{enumerate}

Quantitative evaluation metrics include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Evasion Rate (ER)}: Proportion of flagged pairs pushed below threshold $\tau = 0.75$.
    \item \textbf{Fidelity-Preserved Evasion Rate (FPER)} (Eq. \ref{eq:fper}): Proportion achieving $S(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}') < \tau$ and $\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}') \ge 0.75$.
    \item \textbf{Mean Query Complexity (MQC)}: Average oracle queries consumed per pair ($B \le 50$).
    \item \textbf{Spearman Rank Correlation Decay ($\Delta\rho$)}: Degradation in ranking correlation against original scores.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion \& Future Roadmap}

In this work, we developed an empirical framework analyzing the structural resilience of retrieval and similarity-based plagiarism defenses under adaptive, detector-aware paraphrase attacks. From the historical transition of n-gram string matching to dense transformer representations, similarity architectures have prioritized encoding quality over adversarial queried robustness. By directly targeting the continuous scoring functions of five defense architectures through Leave-One-Out saliency span ablation and independent NLI fidelity gating, we demonstrated that single-vector embeddings suffer severe vulnerabilities ($88.3\%$ FPER), whereas hybrid lexical-semantic and multi-vector late-interaction representations present superior structural stability.

Future extensions of this framework will investigate:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Adversarial Defense Retraining}: Fine-tuning defense encoders on successful Tier 2 evasion texts to close geometric representation holes.
    \item \textbf{Dynamic Query Expansion}: Developing adaptive search protocols that expand query tokens dynamically at retrieval time to capture complex syntactic transformations.
    \item \textbf{Watermark-Guided Similarity Verification}: Integrating cryptographically verifiable watermark signals into retrieval representations to safeguard intellectual property.
\end{enumerate}
